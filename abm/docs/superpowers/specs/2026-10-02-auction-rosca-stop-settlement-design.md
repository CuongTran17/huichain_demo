# Auction ROSCA With Stop-on-Default: Design

## Purpose and boundary

Build an ABM research mode for a six-or-more-member ROSCA in which the highest eligible interest bid receives each period's pot, ETH is locked only when that member receives the pot, and the circle closes at the first default. The purpose is to measure each member's principal loss, interest earned, collateral burden, and settlement delay under ETH shocks and correlated defaults. This is a research simulator, not a safety guarantee or production smart contract.

The existing fixed-order experiment remains available as a separate baseline. The Word draft and current code explicitly describe fixed-order ROSCA; results from that mode must not be labeled as results for the new auction mode. No endogenous bid optimization, strategic coalition formation, loan market, automatic margin call, legal enforcement, or live on-chain execution is part of this version.

## Period mechanics

- `members == periods`; each active member owes the same `contribution` each period, including after receiving a payout. A recipient never bids again. Winning does not change any later scheduled contribution.
- At each due date, detect all defaults and missed contributions for that timestamp **before** collecting that date's contributions. If any occur, freeze the circle without holding that date's auction. Events with the same timestamp form one batch; their input order cannot change settlement.
- Otherwise collect the fixed contribution from every member. Only members who have not received a payout and can lock the required ETH may bid. Bid rates are exogenous scenario inputs, not chosen by a game-theoretic optimizer. Highest eligible rate wins; ties go to the lowest stable member ID. A missing bid is not a zero bid. If no candidate is eligible, close as `no_eligible_bid` and settle; do not invent a recipient.
- A winning bid `b` is a discount on the **gross current-period pot** `P = members × contribution`, with `0 <= b < 1`. Lock ETH atomically before releasing the net payout `P × (1-b)`. Credit `P × b` to an interest ledger retained until closure or scheduled completion. This ledger represents cash already inside the pool, not a new inflow. Prior interest is not used to enlarge a later gross pot.
- Example: six members each contribute 100 USDC; a 20% winning bid makes `P = 600`, net payout 480, and retained interest 120. The recipient pays only 100 USDC at each subsequent due date, not another 20%.

## Collateral and agent state

- The recipient's remaining scheduled obligation immediately after payout is `contribution × (periods - payout_period - 1)` with a zero-based period. Apply either the existing fixed ratio or reputation-adjusted ratio, plus any configured stress floor, to that obligation; divide by the current ETH price to obtain ETH to lock. Before payout the member has no locked ETH. A candidate with insufficient available ETH is skipped, not paid and not marked as defaulted.
- After each later successful 100-USDC contribution, reduce the remaining obligation but retain the full quantity of ETH initially locked. Do not release ETH between periods or automatically rebalance on price changes. At ordinary completion, return all remaining ETH; early closure follows the settlement rules below. This decision supersedes progressive-release requirements in the original implementation plan.
- Each agent has a stable ID, pre-payout contribution balance, cumulative contribution and net payout, payout date, locked ETH, remaining obligation, exogenous available ETH, settlement payment mode, and exogenous USDC capacity at closure. Capacity is a scenario input, not a claim about a real member's income. Preserve the existing reputation state and default-utility function where applicable; also accept explicitly forced, simultaneous defaults for stress tests.
- Default by an already paid member, or failure to contribute before payout, freezes the circle. A never-paid defaulter has no ETH to seize; this version assumes no punitive forfeiture of contributions already made. That assumption must appear in results and can be changed in a later policy study.

## Closure and seven-day settlement

- Freeze auctions, new contributions, and new payouts immediately on the trigger day. The defaulting paid members' ETH is sold immediately at the executable price, after configured slippage, up to their accelerated remaining obligations; any unsold ETH is returned. A defaulter's unfilled obligation is recorded, never imputed as cash.
- Every previously paid, non-defaulting member's remaining scheduled installments become due for terminal settlement. At freeze they choose one of two **exogenous** modes: `eth_first` or `usdc_first`. The former sells only as much ETH as needed immediately, returns ETH not sold, and permits USDC top-up of any shortfall during the grace period. The latter permits payment of the entire obligation in USDC within `grace_days` calendar days and releases all ETH if paid; otherwise any unpaid amount is recovered by selling ETH at the grace deadline, with surplus ETH returned. A scenario supplies each member's USDC capacity and payment day (an integer from freeze day through the deadline); a missing payment day means the deadline. Payment cannot exceed capacity. An unpaid residual becomes a secondary settlement default, not fictional liquidity.
- `grace_days = 7` is the initial scenario value, not an asserted safe optimum. The engine must also support 0, 1, 3, and 14 days for paired sensitivity runs. The daily price path must extend through the latest possible settlement date. For `eth_first`, ETH sale uses the freeze-day execution price; for an unpaid `usdc_first` balance, sale uses the deadline execution price. Optional event-price overrides may represent an intraday wick; ordinary historical runs still use daily closes and must report that limitation.
- The circle is operationally frozen on day 0 but financially settled only after the grace period. Reports must distinguish freeze date, final settlement date, pending USDC, ETH sold, ETH returned, and price used for each sale.

## Claims and cash waterfall

- Keep accounting ledgers for current-period contributions, retained bid interest, USDC received on accelerated obligations, and ETH-sale proceeds. Never count retained interest as both a new deposit and a separate reserve. External reserve funding is disabled in the first auction mode (`reserve_initial = 0`) so the source of any protection is visible.
- A member who has **not** received a payout has a principal claim equal to contributions actually paid. A member who has received a payout has no unpaid-saver principal claim, but may have an accelerated obligation and an interest entitlement. Contributions after payout are repayments, not deposits earning interest.
- At closure, pay unpaid-saver principal claims from all actual USDC available, including bid interest and recovered ETH. Attribute principal repayments from non-interest cash first, then consume retained bid interest only for the uncovered portion; this is attribution of fungible cash, not a second withdrawal. If still insufficient, pay each eligible saver pro rata to their unpaid principal; never create money or assign a negative cash balance. Record each person's principal loss separately. Only a surplus after all principal claims are paid is distributable as interest. At normal completion, distribute retained interest after the final period.
- Divide distributable interest among non-defaulting members by pre-payout `USDC × days-waited`: each pre-payout contribution accrues from its payment date until that member's payout date or the freeze date, whichever comes first. Post-payout contributions earn no weight. A member who received in the first period receives no wait-based interest. If all eligible weights are zero and a positive surplus exists, divide it in proportion to actual pre-payout contributions; if there are no eligible contributions, leave the surplus undistributed and report it explicitly.
- Report separately (1) scheduled obligation not collected, (2) retained interest consumed to protect principal, (3) principal loss per saver, (4) interest actually distributed, and (5) any undistributed balance. A borrower's uncollected obligation need not equal savers' principal loss: for example, after a 480 payout and 120 retained interest, recovering 400 of a 500 obligation still leaves 520 to refund five 100-USDC savers.

## Experiments and acceptance checks

- Reuse the existing Mesa agents, price-path loading, paired fixed-versus-reputation collateral policies, seeds, and CLI conventions where practical. Auction bids, available ETH, forced defaults, payment modes, and USDC capacities are explicit reproducible inputs. The same exogenous inputs and price path are used for both collateral policies; payout order may differ if their collateral eligibility differs and must therefore be reported.
- The first scenario set covers normal completion; first-period default; 2-4 simultaneous paid-member defaults in six members; a 40-50% ETH drawdown before or during settlement; a transient intraday execution-price shock; zero USDC top-up; insufficient ETH; a previously compliant recipient unable to settle within seven days; a missed pre-payout contribution; no eligible bidder; and 0/1/3/7/14-day grace comparisons. These are stress cases, not estimated probabilities.
- In every run assert cash conservation, nonnegative ledgers, at most one payout per member, no ETH lock before payout, no second bid after payout, exactly 100 USDC per later contribution in the six-member example, no interest double count, deterministic same-timestamp default handling, and zero principal loss whenever realized cash covers all unpaid-saver claims.
- Output per-agent payouts, contributions, winning bid, interest entitlement, ETH lock/sale/return, settlement mode, unpaid obligation, principal claim and loss; output per-run freeze/settlement dates, total interest retained/used/distributed, recovered collateral, total losses and worst individual loss. Existing fixed-order CSV schemas remain unchanged; auction outputs are named or versioned distinctly.

## Interpretation and deferred work

### Revision decisions approved on 2026-10-06

- Keep researcher-supplied defaults, fixed scheduled payments and terminal `usdc_capacity`; do not add cash-wallet dynamics or random defaults. Distinguish members triggering the first freeze from other paid members with a positive uncollected terminal obligation. The latter are settlement shortfalls, not later missed-installment events.
- Attribute consumed interest to interest paid by the freeze-triggering group first, then to other members' interest. Within each group allocate pro rata to paid interest. This attributes the existing `interest_used` only; it does not add cash, reduce uncollected obligations or refund winning bids. Report per-member paid interest and both run-level sources.
- Retain highest eligible bid selection, stable ID ties, skipping insufficient ETH and terminal `no_eligible_bid` when no winner exists. Report skipped insufficient-ETH bids separately from defaults. Preserve actual contributions already collected on such a round and the existing settlement waterfall.
- F11 is observational only: at simulated due-date snapshots report locked ETH market value versus remaining obligation, without top-up requests, liquidation triggers or continuous oracle modeling. Coverage for zero obligation is undefined/not applicable. Do not subtract common interest from individual collateral requirements.
- Reuse sale-time `liquidation_slippage`: effective sale price = daily close × (1 − slippage). Historical baseline uses no execution-price overrides and 0% slippage; optional 5%/10% sensitivity values are assumptions, not observed fill data. Disable `stress_drop` for a plain ratio comparison or label any stress-floor scenario separately.
- Keep Streamlit. Candidate ratios must have zero observed principal-loss runs and zero `no_eligible_bid` runs. Report payout counts and completion share separately, including default closures; never infer real-world default/loss probability from exogenously selected scenarios.
- No continuing circle, enforced margin call, cash-wallet model, F12 collateral tie-break, new framework or dependency is included. Implementation tasks are in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-06-auction-risk-accounting-revision.md`.

Do not describe late-recipient interest as guaranteed bank interest. It is funded only by winning bids and may be consumed in a default. A seven-day grace balances human repayment time against ETH-price exposure; compare it empirically rather than calling it safe. Daily close data cannot establish that a brief intraday wick would have been captured by an on-chain oracle or executable sale.

A continuing-circle variant, endogenous bid strategies/game theory, collusion equilibrium, adaptive collateral, oracle implementation, stablecoin depegging, gas/congestion, and contract-security or legal deployment review are separate work. The first auction mode can still stress coordinated defaults with exogenous events.
